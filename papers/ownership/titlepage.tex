\noindent\textbf{This is the second of two companion papers.} The first, \textit{Labor Backing
Accounts: Who Holds the Claims That Wages Pay?}, reports the measurement this paper draws on:
what income services the US claim stock and who holds, guarantees or owes it. This paper asks
whether the tax system can replace the revenue when income moves from labor to capital. Each
stands alone and neither repeats the other.

\vspace{0.5em}

\noindent\textbf{On the title, which has been changed twice and weakened each time.} An early
draft read \textit{Ownership, Not Profit Shifting: Why Capital Taxation Cannot Replace Wage
Taxes}. A later one read \textit{Ownership More Than Profit Shifting: Why Capital Taxation Falls
Short}. Both asserted things that are now known to hold on only one of the two tax objects the
paper measures. Deriving the mapping from the marginal investment wedge to an annual revenue rate
showed that the shortfall closes on the annual object at every corner of the parameter box, and
that the lever ordering between the shareholder base and profit shifting reverses with it. Since
neither half of the old title survives on both objects, the title now names the distinction that
decides the answer. Alternatives: (1) \textit{Which Capital Tax Rate Replaces a Wage Tax?};
(2) \textit{Two Tax Objects, Two Answers on Replacing Wage Revenue}.

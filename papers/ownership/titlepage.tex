\noindent\textbf{This is the second of two companion papers.} The first, \textit{Labor Backing
Accounts: Who Holds the Claims That Wages Pay?}, reports the measurement this paper draws on:
what income services the US claim stock and who holds, guarantees or owes it. This paper asks
whether the tax system can replace the revenue when income moves from labor to capital. Each
stands alone and neither repeats the other.

\vspace{0.5em}


oindent	extbf{On the title, which has been changed three times and weakened each time.} The
drafts read, in order, 	extit{Ownership, Not Profit Shifting: Why Capital Taxation Cannot Replace
Wage Taxes}, then 	extit{Ownership More Than Profit Shifting: Why Capital Taxation Falls Short},
then 	extit{Marginal Wedge or Annual Revenue: Two Answers on Replacing Wage Taxes}. The first two
asserted a shortfall and a lever ordering that hold on only one of the two tax rates the paper
measures. The third led with the marginal-versus-average distinction, and a novelty check with
criteria fixed in advance then established that the distinction is standard public finance and
that the absence of a shortfall is already the finding of a published panel study. Neither could
be presented as ours. The title now says what the paper actually adds, which is the computation.
Alternatives: (1) 	extit{Assembling Both Sides of the Wage-to-Capital Replacement Condition};
(2) 	extit{What Replacing Wage Tax Revenue from Capital Would Require}.

# Which base is the right one, and the case against our answer

Item 6. One paragraph for the argument, one for the counter-argument, stated as fairly as we
can state it.

## The argument for the marginal flow base

What the fiscal condition asks is whether the tax system can replace revenue that stops
arriving as wages and starts arriving as returns to AI capital. That is a question about
**incremental** income: the dollar that moves. The right object is therefore the tax
collected on the next dollar of AI surplus, and it must be inclusive of every layer that
reaches that dollar, because the Treasury does not care which statute collects it. A
marginal, flow-based, all-layers rate is the only construction that answers the question
asked. An average rate on the existing capital stock answers a different question, namely
what the system currently collects from capital as a whole, and it is pushed up by two
things that will not be present on the marginal AI dollar: tax paid on inframarginal returns
that expensing does not shelter, and tax paid on a capital stock most of which is neither
expensed at 100 percent nor readily shiftable. Using it here would flatter the condition by
importing revenue that the displaced wage dollar will not in fact generate.

## The counter-argument, stated fairly

The marginal rate can understate what the state actually collects, for three reasons that
are not weak. First, **expensing is a timing provision, not an exemption**: the deduction is
taken once, and if the AI capital stock stops growing the shelter unwinds and receipts rise
toward the average rate. A condition evaluated on the marginal rate at the moment of fastest
investment is evaluated at the most favourable point in the cycle for the taxpayer and the
least favourable for the Treasury. Second, the fiscal condition is a **stock-flow** question
in practice: the budget must be balanced against the whole capital income base each year, not
against the incremental dollar alone, so the average rate is arguably the relevant one for
the level even if the marginal rate is the relevant one for the direction. Third, the
marginal construction is only as good as its shiftability assumption, and if AI rents prove
**less** shiftable than the multinational average, because they are earned on domestic
customers and domestically held intangible property, then the domestic share is higher than
0.52 and the marginal rate moves toward the average.

**We do not think the counter-argument is resolved by our work.** It is the reason claim 3 is
now reported as a map with a threshold drawn on it rather than as a verdict, and it is the
question we would most want a co-author to attack.

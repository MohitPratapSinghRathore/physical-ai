# Scoping result: the rotation is a denominator artefact

**2026-09-25. KILL-4 was tested first, as `CRITERIA.md` required, because it was free and
used data already built. It has ended the comparative project and corrected sessions 2 and 3.**

---

## Verdict

**The session 3 "rotation" finding is withdrawn. It is an artefact of the labour-backed
denominator, and it reverses on a household-credit denominator.**

**Consequence for this project: the comparative question has lost its subject.** There is no
American rotation to ask whether other states share.

---

## 1. What the test showed

The same federal holder leg, 2007 → 2025, decomposed on two denominators:

| Programme | Labour-backed denominator | Household-credit denominator |
|---|---|---|
| GSE and agency guarantee | **−0.0446** | **+0.1255** |
| Federal direct lending | +0.0282 | +0.0773 |
| Federal Reserve | +0.0341 | 0.0000 |
| **Holder leg total** | **+0.0172** | **+0.2028** |

**The sign flips on the main component.** On household credit the state's guarantee position
rose from **0.3548 to 0.5576** — it grew by a fifth of the household credit stock. On the
labour-backed denominator it looked flat.

## 2. Why

The denominator was inflated by Treasury issuance.

| | 2007 | 2025 | Growth |
|---|---|---|---|
| Household credit | $13,187bn | $18,887bn | **×1.43** |
| Labour-backed claim stock | $15,745bn | $40,380bn | **×2.56** |
| Treasury debt | $6,051bn | $33,887bn | **×5.60** |

The GSE and agency complex grew in dollars and grew as a share of household credit. It shrank
only as a share of a stock that Treasury was expanding underneath it. **Nothing rotated. The
denominator moved.**

## 3. What this does to sessions 2 and 3

**Withdrawn.** Session 3's headline — *"after 2007 the state's guarantee position did not
grow, it rotated"* — is wrong as stated. The holder leg grew **2.71×** in levels over that
period and rose on a household-credit denominator. It was flat only in one share.

**Survives, when restated in levels relative to the denominator.** The two-phase structure is
not itself an artefact:

| | Denominator | Holder leg | Obligor leg |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Phase 1, 1965–2000** | ×20.0 | **×84.6** | ×20.8 |
| **Phase 2, 2007–2025** | ×2.56 | ×2.71 | **×5.92** |

In phase 1 the holder leg grew **four times faster** than the stock it is a share of, while
the obligor leg merely kept pace. In phase 2 the obligor leg grew **2.3 times faster** than
the stock while the holder leg merely kept pace. **That is a real two-phase structure**, and
it is the right way to state it: growth relative to the denominator, not share change, which
is what confused me.

**Also survives:** the U-shape, and that today's level is a return rather than a record.

## 4. What this does to the comparative project

**It stops it.**

The project's question was *"is the rotation American, or do states rotate generally?"* There
is no rotation. Asking whether other countries share a US artefact is not a research question,
and building foreign holder maps to test it would be several sessions spent confirming that a
denominator behaves the way denominators behave.

KILL-1, KILL-2 and KILL-3 were **not tested**, because the order fixed in `CRITERIA.md` put
KILL-4 first precisely so that the expensive checks would not be run on a dissolved premise.
That ordering earned its place.

## 5. The question that is left, and whose it is

There is still a real comparative question: **did other advanced economies' states expand
their position in household credit as the US did, from 0.355 to 0.558 of household credit
between 2007 and 2025?** The natural comparators are the economies with explicit state
mortgage systems — Canada's CMHC, the Netherlands' NHG, Japan's JHF and its predecessor the
Government Housing Loan Corporation.

**But that is a housing-finance question on a household-credit denominator, and the labour
backing accounts add nothing to it.** Per the decision rule in `CRITERIA.md`, this is the case
where "the project is viable but is not a labour backing paper", and the honest thing is to
say so rather than to keep the accounts in the frame because they are ours.

Anyone pursuing it needs household credit by holder, multi-decade, for three or four countries.
That is a real project. It is not this one.

## 6. What I got wrong, and why the check caught it

The error was reporting a **share change** as a statement about behaviour. A share can fall
because the numerator shrank or because the denominator grew, and in a period when Treasury
debt rose 5.6× the denominator was always going to dominate. Sessions 2 and 3 both computed
shares correctly and both described them as if they were quantities.

**What caught it was a kill criterion written to be cheap and tested first.** KILL-4 existed
because the household classes all carry coefficients near 0.8, so the labour-backed and
household-credit denominators were expected to be nearly proportional over household claims.
They are not, because the labour-backed stock also contains Treasury — and that is exactly the
thing that moved.

The lesson for the remaining work is narrow and concrete: **every share in this project should
be reported beside its numerator and denominator growth**, and no share change should be
described in the language of behaviour without that check.

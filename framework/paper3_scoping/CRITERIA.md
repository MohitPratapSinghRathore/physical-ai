# Comparative rotation: scoping criteria, fixed before any search

**2026-09-25. Written before any data or literature was checked.** On branch
`paper2-absorption` in its own directory, because the project is scoped separately from
paper 2 even though it grows out of it.

## The question

Session 3 found that after 2007 the US state's guarantee position **did not grow, it
rotated**: the GSE and agency complex fell 0.0446 as a share of the labour-backed claim
stock while direct federal lending rose 0.0282 and the Federal Reserve rose 0.0341, leaving
the holder leg essentially flat at +0.0172.

**Is that American, or do states rotate generally?**

## What "to the same standard" has to mean

The US holder leg needs three things. Each is a feasibility hurdle abroad:

1. **Claim stock by class** — household mortgage, consumer, government debt, business.
2. **A holder map with the state separable** — who holds or guarantees each class, with
   government, government agencies and the central bank distinguishable from banks.
3. **Labour backing coefficients** — to form the denominator.

**On (3), a simplification is available and is adopted now rather than after seeing results:**
the rotation is a question about the **composition** of the state's position, not its level.
Holding coefficients fixed across countries changes the level and not the composition, so a
structure-only build answers the rotation question. Any level comparison across countries
would need country coefficients and is **out of scope**.

## Kill criteria

**KILL-1, data.** If no advanced economy outside the US has a holder map separating
government, agencies and the central bank for household credit over a multi-decade span, the
comparison cannot be built and the project stops.

**KILL-2, no variation.** If no comparator economy has a state mortgage guarantee or direct
lending system large enough to rotate — say, never above **10 percent** of household credit —
then there is nothing to compare and the US is not distinctive, it is merely alone.

**KILL-3, novelty.** If the comparative history of state housing-finance institutions already
exists quantitatively on a common denominator, the contribution is gone.

**KILL-4, the denominator is doing no work.** Household credit classes all carry labour
backing coefficients near 0.8, so the labour-backed denominator is close to proportional to
household credit. **If the rotation result is identical on a plain household-credit
denominator, then the labour backing accounts add nothing to this paper** and it should be
written as a housing-finance paper by someone else, or not at all. This is tested on the US
data, which is already built, before any foreign data is touched.

## Decision rule

- Any kill fires → report it and stop.
- **KILL-4 fires but the others do not** → the project is viable but is **not a labour
  backing paper**; say so plainly and let the owner decide whether to pursue it on that basis.
- No kill → scope the build: countries, sources, effort, and the realistic target.

**Order of testing, fixed now: KILL-4 first**, because it is free, uses data already built,
and would make the others moot.

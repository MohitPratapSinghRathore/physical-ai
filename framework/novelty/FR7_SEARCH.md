# FR7: systematic novelty search for labor backing accounts

**Run 2026-10-06. Protocol stated before the verdict, kill criterion stated before the search.**

This closes FR7 in the accounts paper, which asked for "a systematic search with a published
protocol for prior constructions of a servicing-income decomposition of a national claim stock."
Until now the paper's claim was "none located", with the explicit admission that no systematic
search had been run.

---

## 1. The object being searched for

A construction that does **all four** of the following:

1. takes the stock of financial claims of a national economy, not one sector and not one
   instrument;
2. classifies each claim by the **income stream that services it**, rather than by instrument,
   counterparty or maturity;
3. crosses that classification with the **holder structure**, so the same claim can be read from
   both sides; and
4. reports the result as an aggregate share of the claim stock.

A construction meeting only some of these is a neighbour, not a precedent, and is recorded as a
neighbour below.

## 2. Kill criterion, fixed before searching

**KILL.** If a prior construction meeting all four conditions is located, the accounts paper's
novelty claim fails. The contribution is then restated as an extension, the prior work is cited
as the origin of the object, and the abstract and introduction are rewritten accordingly.

**NARROW.** If a prior construction meets conditions 1, 2 and 4 but not 3, or covers one sector
only, the claim narrows to the specific combination and the prior work is cited as the nearest
neighbour.

**SURVIVE.** If neither fires, the claim stays "none located", but it may now be stated as the
result of a search with a published protocol rather than as an admission that none was run.

## 3. Queries

Six searches, each recorded with what it was aimed at.

| # | Query | Aimed at |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | decomposition of national debt stock by income source that services it, wage income, financial accounts measurement | the object stated directly |
| 2 | "labor backed" OR "wage-backed" claims, financial accounts, share of debt serviced from wages | the object under our own name and its obvious synonyms |
| 3 | classify financial claims by income stream servicing them, who-to-whom holder structure crossed | conditions 2 and 3 together |
| 4 | government exposure to labor income, contingent liabilities, guarantees, share of claims serviced by wages | the fiscal-risk literature, where it would live if it existed there |
| 5 | distributional national accounts, liabilities side, who owes debt by income type | the distributional national accounts literature |
| 6 | "share of debt" "serviced by" wages, aggregate, public and private | the headline statistic itself |

## 4. What was located, and why none of it is a precedent

**Debt service ratios.** The BIS database of debt service ratios and the Federal Reserve's
household debt service ratio measure a *flow*, required payments against income, for the
household sector, against *disposable* income. Our object is a *stock* decomposition, across all
sectors, against *labor* income specifically. The paper already names the Federal Reserve ratio
as the closest published statistic and the search confirms that judgement rather than displacing
it. Neighbour, not precedent: fails conditions 1, 3 and 4.

**Distributional national accounts.** Piketty, Saez and Zucman distribute household wealth, and
impute mortgage and other debt, across the income distribution. That answers how much each group
owns and owes. It does not classify claims by the income that services them, and it does not
cross that classification with the holder structure. Neighbour: fails conditions 2 and 3.

**Contingent liability frameworks.** Polackova's fiscal risk matrix, Cebotari's measurement
practice and the GAO and rating-agency inventories of US government obligations classify an
obligation by whether the government must pay it and under what trigger. That is a different
cut, and it covers the government's own obligations rather than the whole claim structure. The
paper says this in its related work. Neighbour: fails conditions 1 and 2.

**Who-to-whom classifications.** The IMF's balance of payments and monetary and financial
statistics manuals classify financial assets and liabilities by instrument and counterparty
sector. This is the structure our accounts extend; it carries no servicing-income dimension.
Neighbour: fails condition 2.

**Securitization.** Claims backed by identified income streams are standard at the instrument
level, and that is where the vocabulary of "backing" comes from. No national aggregate is built
from it. Neighbour: fails conditions 1 and 4.

**Household debt distribution.** A 2025 article on the distribution of household debt in the
United States, 1950 to 2022, sits alongside Bartscher, Kuhn, Schularick and Steins, which the
paper already cites. Household sector only, and distributional rather than servicing-income.
Neighbour: fails conditions 1, 2 and 3.

## 5. Verdict

**SURVIVE.** Neither the kill nor the narrow criterion fires. No construction meeting all four
conditions was located.

What changes is the standing of the claim, not the claim itself. The paper may now say that a
search with a published protocol located no prior construction, and name the nearest neighbours
and the condition each fails. It may **not** say that none exists. Six queries against a general
web index is a documented search, not an exhaustive one: it does not cover paywalled journal
archives systematically, non-English literature, statistical agency working papers that are not
web-indexed, or unpublished central bank work, and any of those could hold a precedent.

## 6. What this does to the paper

- L7 is rewritten: the limitation is no longer "no systematic search has been run" but "the
  search that was run has stated bounds", with those bounds given.
- FR7 is rewritten, since the avenue it proposed has now been taken; what remains is the
  coverage this search does not reach.
- The related-work section gains the condition each neighbour fails, which is more useful to a
  referee than a list of what was not found.

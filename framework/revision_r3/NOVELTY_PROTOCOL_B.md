# NOVELTY_PROTOCOL_B: kill criteria for the restated Paper B, fixed before searching

**2026-10-08. Written and committed before any search is run.**

Paper B was restructured around a distinction between two tax objects without a novelty gate.
Paper C's reframing got one and B's did not, which is an inconsistency in our own process.
This closes it, on the same terms: criteria first, searches second, verdict against the criteria
whatever it says.

## What the restated paper claims

1. **The mapping.** In a stationary state expensing and economic depreciation give the same
   annual deduction, so the annual entity base is all of net capital income and not rents
   alone; the rent share drops out of the annual object; and the entity tax that washes out on
   a marginal project does not wash out on the annual flow from capital in place.
2. **The consequence for the replacement question.** A displaced wage dollar's replacement
   condition answered with a marginal wedge fails and answered with an annual rate closes, and
   the gap between the objects, 13.2 points federally, exceeds every disagreement among the
   twelve assembled components.
3. **The shareholder-layer decomposition.** Splitting the non-taxable share by treatment gives
   23.2 percent in traditional retirement arrangements, untaxed on the margin and taxed at
   ordinary rates on distribution, 42 percent held abroad bearing withholding on dividends, and
   only 8.8 percent never reached by any federal tax, against a headline figure of about 73
   percent "in forms the individual income tax barely reaches".

## Kill criteria, fixed now

> **KILL-1, the mapping is textbook.** If the distinction between a marginal effective tax rate
> and an average or annual effective rate, including that expensing nulls the former on normal
> returns while the latter stays positive on existing capital, is established in the public
> finance literature, then claim 1 is not a contribution. Preliminary reading already suggests
> this fires. It is written as a criterion anyway so the verdict is on the record.

> **KILL-2, the target is our own.** If no published work makes the claim that capital taxation
> cannot replace revenue lost from displaced wages, computed on a marginal wedge, then Paper B
> corrects an error this project introduced. That is a corrigendum to our own earlier draft and
> not a paper, and the current manuscript is withdrawn rather than retitled.

> **KILL-3, the decomposition exists.** If published work already decomposes the non-taxable
> share of US corporate equity by eventual tax treatment and reports that the share beyond all
> federal tax is far below the headline tax-exempt share, claim 3 is not a contribution either
> and nothing survives.

> **PROCEED AS A NOTE.** KILL-1 and KILL-2 both fire but KILL-3 does not. The analytic framing
> is withdrawn; what remains is a short empirical note on who bears the US shareholder layer,
> built on claim 3, with the marginal-versus-annual point cited as the known distinction it is
> and used only to motivate the measurement.

> **PROCEED AS A PAPER.** KILL-2 does not fire, meaning the shortfall claim is made in print by
> others on a marginal-wedge basis. Then correcting it is a contribution even though the
> distinction is textbook, because the correction overturns a published conclusion. The paper is
> then framed as a correction of that specific literature and must cite the claim it corrects.

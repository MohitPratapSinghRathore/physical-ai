\section{Institutions, by regime}
\label{sec:institutions}

This section draws out what follows for institutions from the measurements above. It is
organised around one conclusion, and it is careful to separate what the evidence supports from
what it does not, because a paper of this kind is read for its recommendations and the
recommendations are the easiest part to overreach.

\subsection{The organising conclusion}

\begin{quote}
The state loses in both failure directions, through different tax bases: wage taxes if AI
succeeds, capital gains and corporate taxes if it fails. The same capital tax rate is therefore
the state's only claim on the AI upside and the thing that would have to rise to close the fiscal
condition after displacement. It is being asked to do two opposite jobs.
\end{quote}

That is why the capital tax instrument is the only one that appears in the minimum sufficient set
in both directions, and it is why the contested base in Section~\ref{sec:fiscal} is the hinge of
everything here rather than a technical footnote. It is a structural observation about who holds
what, not a forecast about which direction events will take.

\subsection{What is needed at each regime}

Four displacement regimes and the failure case call for different instruments, and the useful
statement is the minimum sufficient set at each rather than a list of everything that might help.

\paragraph{Inside the data, about ten percent of the wage bill.} The tax instrument, the trust
fund contribution base, the student loan trigger, and better measurement. That is the set. The
housing agencies absorb this level without a Treasury draw, so mortgage instruments are not needed
here; ownership stakes are not needed here; the banking system barely moves, so a supervisory
scenario is a disclosure requirement rather than a binding constraint. This is the only regime
fully inside the observed data, and it is the one where the paper can say what is unnecessary as
confidently as what is necessary.

\paragraph{Larger displacement, twenty-five to fifty percent.} Nearly everything, and the
composition changes rather than just the scale. The break-even capital tax rate, which is
\result{BreakEvenALow} to \result{BreakEvenAHigh} percent with output preserved and
\result{BreakEvenBLow} to \result{BreakEvenBHigh} percent with output falling, reaches the top of
the range of effective rates anyone has measured. Instruments that act on ownership and on direct
claims enter here, precisely because the tax instrument is at its limit. All of this is scenario
and outside the data for the reemployment rate.

\paragraph{Sudden displacement.} The same set reordered. Only the contract instruments act inside
a quarter: forbearance protocols, income-driven repayment, payment-contingent clauses. Tax changes
and ownership stakes take legislative time the regime does not have, and pre-funding is useless
once the shock has arrived. Pre-authorization is the whole instrument here; the precedent that
worked was written in weeks because the protocol already existed.

\paragraph{Near-total displacement.} The share of displaced wages re-earned falls toward zero, so
the required capital tax rate approaches the whole effective labor tax rate. Every revenue-side
instrument that taxes labor income is arithmetically empty when there is no labor income. Only
holding the asset works. This regime is where the question stops being financial regulation and
becomes a question about ownership, and the honest thing for this paper to do is to say so and
stop.

\paragraph{The failure case.} Instruments that protect the wage tax base do nothing here.
Concentration limits on AI-linked lending and the capital tax rate are what remain, and the second
one is the same row that appears in every displacement regime, which is the conclusion above
restated as a table entry.

\subsection{What ownership would cost}

Because ownership is the instrument that survives at the extreme, its price is worth stating. The
AI side at the central scale is about \result{AISideTotalBn} billion dollars. Raising the state's
share of it from about \result{AIFederalShareRounded} percent to ten percent would require a stake
of roughly \result{StakeForTenPctBn} billion dollars and would close about
\result{GapClosedAtTenPct} percent of the gap between the two sides. For scale, that stake is
about \result{StakeVersusAgencyCapital} times the entire capital of the housing agencies.

That arithmetic is why most of the instruments in the appendix table are not ownership
instruments. It is also why the ownership row cannot be dismissed: at near-total displacement it is
the only row left, and the cost of acquiring a position rises with the price of the asset, which is
exactly what a successful AI transition would do to it.

\subsection{What each type of institution should change}

Each recommendation below is tied to a measured exposure in Section~\ref{sec:banks}, and each
carries what the evidence does not support, because the second half is what keeps the first half
honest.

Card-heavy lenders are the most exposed model by a distance and should hold capital against the
card book for a displacement scenario specifically. What is not supported is treating this as a
tail risk: it is the first model to break and it breaks inside the range this paper models. Credit
unions are the only class with stress at the level inside the data, because a book that is almost
entirely household credit has nothing to dilute a wage shock with; what is not supported is
treating them as small and therefore safe. Large and regional banks should look to the supervisory
scenario routed through the second round; what is not supported is the idea that household
underwriting change helps them, since their exposure is not their household book. Auto lenders
should write displacement-contingent payment clauses into contracts and securitization
documentation; what is not supported is wholesale change at moderate displacement, where they show
no breaches at all. Housing agencies should pre-authorize forbearance and raise credit enhancement
on the unenhanced part of the book; what is not supported is recapitalization, since no Treasury
draw appears at any level we ran. Insurers should report their AI-linked and wage-linked holdings
together, because they are the population most exposed to both sides at once; what is not supported
is forced-sale style rules, since they hold to maturity against matched liabilities. For private
credit the instrument is disclosure sufficient to separate it from the aggregate it currently sits
inside, and that is a measurement instrument rather than a prudential one; no sizing claim is
supported, because we do not know the number. Pension funds should report the funding gap against a
displacement path rather than only against asset returns. State and local governments should build
reserves against a wage displacement path specifically, and should not assume the federal pattern,
because the balanced-budget constraint turns their revenue fall into a procyclical spending cut
that feeds the same demand channel.

\subsection{What the evidence does not support}

Two claims are worth stating negatively, in full, because both are tempting and neither survives
the measurement.

\paragraph{Wholesale change to household mortgage underwriting at moderate displacement.} Three
measured reasons. The flat debt-service result is partly mechanical, since underwriting caps
debt service to income in the first place. Physically exposed households are less indebted on most
of the measures we can compute. And mortgage portfolio lenders are among the least exposed
institution types at institution level, which closes at the level of the balance sheet a question
that occupational data alone left open.

\paragraph{Occupation-based underwriting, on the legal question as well as the empirical one.}
This is stated carefully because an earlier and looser version of it was wrong. Occupation is not a
prohibited basis under the Equal Credit Opportunity Act; the regulation's list of prohibited bases
does not include it, and a creditor may consider information that is not used to discriminate on a
prohibited basis. The exposure is different in kind: because occupation correlates with protected
classes, occupation-based underwriting in housing credit exposes a lender to a
\emph{discriminatory-effects} claim under the Fair Housing Act, on which the lender must carry a
legally sufficient justification under a burden-shifting framework. Combined with the measured
finding that the exposure is spread almost evenly across borrowers, so that screening on it buys a
lender very little, the risk-adjusted case for occupation-based underwriting is weak and the case
for portfolio-level disclosure of occupational concentration is strong.

\subsection{Emerging markets}

Three facts change the answer outside a reserve-currency economy, and they compound.

If AI capital is imported, the taxable surplus accrues abroad, so the domestic capital tax rate on
it is effectively lower than the already low rate measured in Section~\ref{sec:fiscal}. The tax
instrument is weakest exactly where it is needed most. A sovereign that cannot borrow in a
currency it issues cannot absorb the shock the way the United States can, which leaves pre-funding
as the only instrument that works and pre-funding has to be built before the event. And many such
economies are already on a steep path: the twenty-year baseline in our debt module reaches
\result{EMBaseline} percent of output before any displacement is added, and the displacement
increment at the ten percent level is \result{EMIncrementLow} to \result{EMIncrementHigh} points of
output against \result{ReserveIncrementLow} to \result{ReserveIncrementHigh} for a reserve-currency
issuer, a ratio of about \result{EMRatio}.

The measurement itself is also unavailable in those economies, and that is a finding rather than an
excuse. For the one case we attempted in detail, the flow of funds data have long lags and
incomplete household coverage, there is no usable counterparty matrix, and high informality places
a large share of labor income outside both the tax base and the formal credit system, which is
precisely the variable the statistic turns on. The honest output there is a statement about why,
not a number.

\subsection{State-contingent triggers}

The instruments above are more useful attached to a trigger than left as advice, and each trigger
should carry its current value and a label saying whether the threshold sits inside the observed
data. Six are worth stating in the body.

The capital tax trigger is already tripped: the assembled rate is \result{TauKBarkai} percent
against a threshold of \result{RequiredEasy} percent, and both sides of that comparison are
observed today. Prime-age nonemployment stands at \result{PrimeAgeNonemp} against the observed
maximum of \result{PrimeAgeMax}, which is the point at which the reemployment fit leaves the data;
crossing it is precisely the moment the rest of this architecture can no longer be sized. The
observed annual displacement flow is \result{DisplacementFlow}, inside the range of speed limits
our specifications imply, which is a range and never a single number. The debt-financed share of
AI capital spending is \result{DebtFinancedShare} percent against the twenty percent read off the
equity-led historical episode, and it is a lower bound for the reason given in
Section~\ref{sec:bust}. Auto delinquency stands at \result{AutoDelinquency} percent. And the
combined social insurance depletion date is \result{OASDIDepletion}, which arrives before any
plausible AI displacement path does, so the trust fund instrument binds soonest for reasons that
have nothing to do with AI.

One trigger matters more than the others and it is a statement about knowledge rather than about
the economy. Past a displacement level of about forty-six percent of the physically exposed wage
bill, the reemployment construction has no admissible solution, and no reemployment-based
instrument can be sized from these data at all. The architecture does not become wrong there. It
becomes unsizeable, which is a different and more honest kind of limit.

Two indicators sit outside our own measurement and inside somebody else's, and they are the ones
to watch for the channel this engine cannot see: recent graduate unemployment at
\result{GradUnemployment} percent and underemployment at \result{GradUnderemployment} percent,
which speak to entry that does not happen rather than to separations that do.

# Pre-registration: second-round effects and the order of stress

Registered **before** running the second-round module (item 4) and the rebuilt order of
stress (item 5). Written 2026-09-19. Owner-specified.

## The registered expectation

> At **moderate displacement** (under about 25 percent of the total wage bill) only the
> public budget and the trust funds cross, **in both columns**.
>
> At **large displacement** the **first-round column still shows little bank stress**, while
> the **second-round column crosses** through house prices, spending and business credit.

## What would refute it

- Any private balance sheet crossing in EITHER column below 25 percent of the total wage bill.
- The first-round column crossing for banks at large displacement, which would mean the
  first-round channel is larger than A75 reported.
- The second-round column NOT crossing at large displacement, which would mean the demand
  channel adds nothing.
- The two columns agreeing throughout, which would mean the first-round and second-round
  distinction does not matter and A75 stands as written.

## Status of A75 while this runs

**A75's "no private balance sheet crosses at any level" is PROVISIONAL and must not be
quoted.** Item 1 of this session is a hand check of the high-displacement cells specifically
because that conclusion is surprising and the credit side may saturate above the old
top-quintile grid the way the fiscal columns were shown to.

## Scope of the household engine, to be attached to every result

The engine measures **first-round losses only**: displaced households defaulting on their own
obligations. It holds house prices, consumer demand, business revenue and the employment of
non-displaced workers **fixed**, and it uses a default factor estimated in ordinary labour
markets. The Federal Reserve's 708 billion dollars comes from an **economy-wide** scenario in
which all of those move together. **Comparing the two is a lower bound on bank losses, not an
estimate**, and every comparison in this repository must say so.

## ADDENDUM, registered 2026-09-19, owner-specified, BEFORE running item 4

Items 4f to 4j are added to the second-round module. All are SCENARIO, labelled as such,
reported as bands.

**4f. Demand and who buys the output.** Income shifts from wages to capital. Using sourced
marginal propensities to consume out of labour income and out of capital income (Mian,
Straub and Sufi, to be verified before citation), estimate the fall in aggregate consumption
for each scenario with output held constant. Translate the shortfall into business revenue
loss and, through the Federal Reserve mapping only, into business credit and commercial real
estate losses and second-round layoffs of non-displaced workers.

**4g. Prices and real debt burdens.** For price and wage declines of 5, 10 and 20 percent
with debts fixed in nominal terms, give the change in debt service to income for mortgaged
households and the implied change in default probability. Debt deflation literature: Fisher,
plus a modern treatment, both to be verified.

**4h. Credit supply.** Lenders tighten before defaults arrive. A labelled sensitivity in
which mortgage credit to high-exposure areas contracts and feeds into the house price path,
with sources if available.

**4i. Government debt dynamics.** For each scenario, the annual fiscal loss with and without
outlays, carried into a path of federal debt to GDP and interest cost over 10 and 20 years
under stated interest rate and growth assumptions, against the CBO baseline if reachable and
otherwise a stated baseline. Reported separately for a reserve-currency issuer and, as a
labelled contrast, for an emerging market that cannot borrow freely in its own currency.

**4j. Two worlds, side by side.** NO POLICY RESPONSE (the wage-based architecture left as it
is) and WITH RESPONSE (the instruments in framework/architecture.md: taxing the surplus at
the break-even rate, replacement income funded from it, displacement-contingent debt relief).
Which losses the response removes and which it does not. Output is preserved in these
scenarios, so the losses are distributional failures, not resource shortages.

### The registered expectation for 4f to 4j, written before running

> Under NO POLICY RESPONSE, large displacement produces losses on bank books and public debt
> paths **comparable to or beyond the Federal Reserve's severely adverse scenario**, driven
> **mainly by demand, house prices and business credit** rather than by displaced borrowers'
> own loans. WITH RESPONSE, most of that is avoided.

### What would refute it

- The demand channel turning out smaller than the direct channel at large displacement.
- The no-response bank losses staying well inside the severely adverse scenario at every dose.
- The policy response leaving most of the loss in place, which would mean the instruments do
  not address the channel that actually does the damage.

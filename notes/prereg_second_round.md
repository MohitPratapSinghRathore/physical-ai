# Pre-registration: second-round effects and the order of stress

Registered **before** running the second-round module (item 4) and the rebuilt order of
stress (item 5). Written 2026-09-19. Owner-specified.

## The registered expectation

> At **moderate displacement** (under about 25 percent of the total wage bill) only the
> public budget and the trust funds cross, **in both columns**.
>
> At **large displacement** the **first-round column still shows little bank stress**, while
> the **second-round column crosses** through house prices, spending and business credit.

## What would refute it

- Any private balance sheet crossing in EITHER column below 25 percent of the total wage bill.
- The first-round column crossing for banks at large displacement, which would mean the
  first-round channel is larger than A75 reported.
- The second-round column NOT crossing at large displacement, which would mean the demand
  channel adds nothing.
- The two columns agreeing throughout, which would mean the first-round and second-round
  distinction does not matter and A75 stands as written.

## Status of A75 while this runs

**A75's "no private balance sheet crosses at any level" is PROVISIONAL and must not be
quoted.** Item 1 of this session is a hand check of the high-displacement cells specifically
because that conclusion is surprising and the credit side may saturate above the old
top-quintile grid the way the fiscal columns were shown to.

## Scope of the household engine, to be attached to every result

The engine measures **first-round losses only**: displaced households defaulting on their own
obligations. It holds house prices, consumer demand, business revenue and the employment of
non-displaced workers **fixed**, and it uses a default factor estimated in ordinary labour
markets. The Federal Reserve's 708 billion dollars comes from an **economy-wide** scenario in
which all of those move together. **Comparing the two is a lower bound on bank losses, not an
estimate**, and every comparison in this repository must say so.

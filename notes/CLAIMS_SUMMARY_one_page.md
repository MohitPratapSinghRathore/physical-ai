# Who holds the claims that wages pay

One page for a prospective co-author. 2026-09-20. Detail and every caveat:
`notes/CLAIMS_DOSSIER_full.md`. To rebuild the two headline constructions from scratch:
`notes/replication_brief_v2_2_addendum.md`. Tags: **[M]** measured, **[S]** scenario (conditional on an
assumed path), **[R]** independently rebuilt from raw data and a published brief by an
instance that did not write the code. **Nothing from the institution or AI-bust work is
tagged [R].**

---

**1. Labour backing accounts.** We introduce a decomposition of all US financial claims by
the income that services them and by who ultimately holds them. It yields a ratio, a sovereign
share, a holder map, a 1952 to 2025 series and a breakdown by wage quintile. **About half of
all US debt, public and private, is serviced directly from wages** (52 percent), 60 percent
including debt paid indirectly once wages become spending and spending becomes business
revenue. **Government debt enters by the labour-linked share of the receipts that service it,
not because a household owes it.** On all claims including equity the pair is 27 and 47
percent; equity is 48 percent of that denominator and moves with prices, so the debt-only
measure is the stable one. **The federal government is exposed, as holder, guarantor or
debtor, on about 79 percent of the directly wage-backed claims**: about 32 percent as creditor
or guarantor and 55 percent as the debtor on debt serviced from wage taxes. The share traces a
**U**: 52 percent in 1952, 34 in 1970, 79 now, and **much of the recent rise is federal debt
growth rather than new exposure to households**. Conventions: exclude what the government owes
and it is 32 percent; treat agency pools as not federal, 59; count indirectly wage-backed
claims, 45. **[M]**, sovereign share agreed to within 0.016 by an independent rebuild;
debt-only ratio **[M], provisional**.

**2. Displacement is fiscal before it is financial.** At 10 percent of wages displaced, the
only level fully inside observed data, the federal government bears **80 to 90 percent** of
first-round losses and direct household credit losses are small. When automation works
through **non-hiring** rather than layoffs, losses shift onto younger borrowers with student
and car debt and **the fiscal loss is unchanged to the cent**. Savings buffers are thinnest
in the **second** wage quintile; differences between physically and cognitively exposed
households are a **pay effect**. **[M]**

**3. The budget does not absorb it, and the verdict is robust to everything still
unsourced.** The condition needs **11 to 14 percent**. Rebuilt from components, counting every
tax layer that reaches the marginal dollar once, the rate on AI surplus is **8.6 percent**
under the Barkai rent reading (7.3 to 9.9) and **1.5 percent** under Karabarbounis and Neiman.
**Both are below the requirement**, it passes in **0.1 percent** of the remaining parameter
space against the easier labour-tax reading and **nowhere** against the harder, and **no
single remaining parameter can cross that line alone.** The reason is ownership, and it is now
measured on three instruments: **most corporate equity and debt is held in forms the tax
system barely reaches.** Only **27 percent** of US corporate equity sits in taxable accounts
(42 percent foreign, 25 percent in retirement accounts); **47 percent** of capital gains are
held until death and never taxed; **33 percent** of corporate interest is sheltered in
retirement accounts while the rest of the world holds **29 percent** of the bonds. Sourcing
those moved our rate down **3.1 points**; **profit shifting moves it 1.2.** The earlier **7
percent** omitted shareholder tax and the IMF's **20 to 22** is an average on the existing
stock; both are kept as marked points. **[R]** rent share and expensing, **[M]** the base
parameters, **[S]** the assembled rate.

**4. Banks are reached indirectly, and household relief does not protect them.** Measured
across **8,612 balance sheets** (4,313 banks, 4,299 credit unions). At 10 percent the system
barely moves: **0.04 percent of assets** below minimums against a 0.03 baseline. Breaches open
above 25 percent; one year of earnings cuts the 50 percent figure to about a third.
**Card-heavy lenders are most exposed, mortgage portfolio lenders least. Forbearance,
income-driven repayment and wage insurance together remove under 10 percent of bank losses at
every level**, because bank losses are mostly second-round: at 10 percent, 27bn of 205bn is
first-round. Enhanced wage insurance costs **314bn to remove 12.9bn**. **Judge these
instruments on household outcomes**; they still cut assets in breach from 4.02 to 1.48 percent
at 25 percent. Balance sheets **[M]**; losses above the inside-data level **[S]**.

**5. An AI bust is a fiscal event too, and the financing verdict is conditional.** A bust
costs **569 to 955bn** in capital gains and corporate tax receipts, while its wage-side effect
is **12 to 80 times weaker** than displacement. It transmits weakly to spending because the
**top 1 percent hold 50.9 percent of corporate equity**. The structure "resembles 2000, not
2008" **only on the nine filers' own books**: the verdict flips on **66bn** of additional
debt-financed capex against about **162bn of AI-linked bank lending already drawn**, and
off-balance-sheet financing is unsourced. **[S]**, historical receipts falls **[M]**.

**6. One tax rate, two opposite jobs.** *A structural observation, not a forecast.* The state loses in **both** failure directions
through **different tax bases**: wage taxes if AI succeeds, capital gains and corporate taxes
if it fails. In the payoff table only the federal government and banks lose in every column;
households gain under success because they hold 38 percent of the AI side against 6 percent
of the wage side. **The same capital tax rate is the state's only claim on the AI upside and
the thing that would have to rise to close the budget after displacement.** **[S]**

**7. What each institution type should do** is set out by regime and by type in
`framework/architecture.md`: tax authority, Treasury, social insurance, housing agencies,
student loan system, auto lenders, supervisors, insurers, pensions, private credit, state and
local government, emerging markets. **Bank protection comes from capital, earnings retention,
the supervisory scenario and concentration limits, not from household relief.**

---

**Limitations.** Off-balance-sheet and GPU-backed AI financing unmeasured, so the AI side is
a lower bound. Realised capital gains are **6.2 to 15.4 percent of AGI** (IRS SOI 2021 to
2023) and bear preferential rates, so the labour-linked receipts share is a **bound, 0.634 to
0.653**; the sovereign share moves under 1 percent across it. **The one-step rule is the
largest judgement in the project**: it moves the all-claims ratio 76 percent, the debt-only
ratio 15. **Novelty is "none located", not "none exists".** A quarter of the mortgage book is
an unidentified residual. **The bottom-quintile debt magnitude is withdrawn; the gradient
stands.** Credit losses are first-round with house prices fixed, so they are floors; above
about 25 percent displacement estimates become bands, a limit of our method, not the world.
Institution dispersion reflects business mix only. The propensity to consume out of stock
wealth is **sourced and verified** at 3.2 cents (Chodorow-Reich, Nenov and Simsek 2021, AER
111(5)); the credit-led GDP path is still understated because bank-channel damage is not fed
back. In the tax assembly the debt share is the **economy-wide corporate stock ratio standing
in for a marginal flow**; Module B points below it, which would harden the verdict.

**The question for you.** It is no longer a parameter. The rate on the marginal dollar of US
AI profit, assembled with every layer counted once, is **8.6 percent** against a requirement
of 11 to 14, and **no input we have left moves it across that line.** What is genuinely open
is the **base**: whether a marginal flow rate is the right object at all, against an average
rate on the existing stock, which is where the IMF's 20 to 22 comes from. The case each way
is in `framework/tau_k/base_argument.md`, and nothing in our work settles it.

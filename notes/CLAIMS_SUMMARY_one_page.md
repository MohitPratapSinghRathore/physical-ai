# Who holds the claims that wages pay

One page for a prospective co-author. 2026-09-20. Detail, sources and every caveat:
`notes/CLAIMS_DOSSIER_full.md`. Tags: **[M]** measured, **[S]** scenario (conditional on an
assumed path), **[R]** independently rebuilt from raw data and a published brief by an
instance that did not write the code. **Nothing from the institution or AI-bust work is
tagged [R].**

---

**1. Labour backing accounts.** We introduce a decomposition of all US financial claims by
the income that services them and by who ultimately holds them. It yields a ratio, a
sovereign share, a holder map, a 1952 to 2025 series and a breakdown by wage quintile.
**About half of all US debt, public and private, is serviced directly out of wages** (52 percent), 60 percent including debt paid indirectly. Government debt enters by the labour-linked share of the receipts that service it, not because a household owes it, 60 percent including debt
paid indirectly, once wages become spending and spending becomes business revenue. On all
claims including equity the pair is 27 and 47 percent; equity is 48 percent of that
denominator and moves with prices, so the debt-only measure is the stable one. **The federal
government is exposed, as holder, guarantor or debtor, on about 79 percent of the directly
wage-backed claims: about 32 percent as creditor or guarantor and about 55 percent as the
debtor on debt serviced from wage taxes.** The share traces a **U**: 52 percent in 1952,
almost all wartime Treasury debt, 34 percent in 1970, 79 percent now. The agency book grew
1970 to 1990; federal debt grew 2008 to 2020, and **much of the recent rise is that debt
growth rather than new exposure to households**. Conventions, stated: exclude what the
government owes and it is 32 percent; treat agency pools as not federal, 59; count
indirectly wage-backed claims, 45. **[M]**, sovereign share agreed to within 0.016 by an
independent rebuild; debt-only ratio **[M], provisional**.

**2. Displacement is fiscal before it is financial.** At 10 percent of wages displaced, the
only level fully inside observed data, the federal government bears **80 to 90 percent** of
first-round losses and direct household credit losses are small. When automation works
through **non-hiring** rather than layoffs, losses shift onto younger borrowers with student
and car debt and **the fiscal loss is unchanged to the cent**. Savings buffers are thinnest
in the **second** wage quintile; differences between physically and cognitively exposed
households are a **pay effect**. **[M]**

**3. Whether the budget absorbs it depends on which capital tax rate reaches AI profits.**
The condition needs about **11 to 14 percent**. AI capital, after immediate expensing and
profit shifting, bears about **7**. Capital economy-wide bears **20 to 22**. So **a quarter
to a half of AI profits would have to be taxed at the ordinary rate**, which needs no
statutory rate rise. **[R]** on the inputs; the verdict is **provisional**, because it turns
on which tax base is right.

**4. Banks are reached indirectly, and household relief does not protect them.** Measured
across **8,612 balance sheets** (4,313 banks, 4,299 credit unions). At 10 percent the system
barely moves: **0.04 percent of assets** below minimums against a baseline of 0.03. Breaches
open above 25 percent. One year of earnings cuts the 50 percent figure to about a third.
**Card-heavy lenders are most exposed; mortgage portfolio lenders among the least.**
**Forbearance, income-driven student repayment and wage insurance together remove under 10
percent of bank losses at every level**, because bank losses are mostly second-round: at 10
percent, 27bn of 205bn is first-round. Enhanced wage insurance costs **314bn to remove 12.9bn**
of bank losses. **Judge these instruments on household outcomes.** They still cut assets in
breach from 4.02 to 1.48 percent at 25 percent. Balance sheets **[M]**; losses above the
inside-data level **[S]**.

**5. An AI bust is a fiscal event too, and the financing verdict is conditional.** A bust
costs **569 to 955bn** in capital gains and corporate tax receipts, while its wage-side effect
is **12 to 80 times weaker** than displacement. It transmits weakly to spending because the
**top 1 percent hold 50.9 percent of corporate equity**. The structure "resembles 2000, not
2008" **only on the nine filers' own books**: the verdict flips on **66bn** of additional
debt-financed capex against about **162bn of AI-linked bank lending already drawn**, and
off-balance-sheet financing is unsourced. **[S]**, historical receipts falls **[M]**.

**6. One tax rate, two opposite jobs.** *A structural observation, not a forecast.* The state loses in **both** failure directions
through **different tax bases**: wage taxes if AI succeeds, capital gains and corporate taxes
if it fails. In the payoff table only the federal government and banks lose in every column;
households gain under success because they hold 38 percent of the AI side against 6 percent
of the wage side. **The same capital tax rate is the state's only claim on the AI upside and
the thing that would have to rise to close the budget after displacement.** **[S]**

**7. What each institution type should do** is set out by regime and by type in
`framework/architecture.md`: tax authority, Treasury, social insurance, housing agencies,
student loan system, auto lenders, supervisors, insurers, pensions, private credit, state and
local government, emerging markets. **Bank protection comes from capital, earnings retention,
the supervisory scenario and concentration limits, not from household relief.**

---

**Limitations.** Off-balance-sheet and GPU-backed AI financing unmeasured, so the AI side is
a lower bound. Realised capital gains are **6.2 to 15.4 percent of AGI** (IRS SOI 2021 to 2023) and bear preferential rates, so the labour-linked receipts share is a **bound, 0.634 to 0.653**, not a point; the sovereign share moves under 1 percent across it. **The one-step rule is the
largest judgement in the project**: it moves the all-claims ratio 76 percent and the
debt-only ratio 15. **Novelty is "none located", not "none exists"**, pending a systematic
search. A quarter of the mortgage book is an unidentified residual. **The bottom-quintile
debt magnitude is withdrawn; the gradient stands.** All credit losses are first-round with
house prices fixed, so they are floors. Above about 25 percent displacement estimates become
bands, which is a limit of our method, not the world. In the institution work, dispersion
reflects business mix only, so it is a lower bound. In the bust work, the propensity to
consume out of wealth is an assumption checked against two episodes, not a sourced parameter,
and the credit-led GDP path is understated because bank-channel damage is not fed back.

**The question for you.** Is about 7 percent the right rate, or the wrong base? We compute
the rate on the marginal dollar of US AI profit: 21 percent statutory, the normal return
exempted by immediate expensing, 48 percent of remaining rents booked offshore. The IMF
measures 20 to 22 percent economy-wide including personal taxes on dividends and gains. The
condition needs 11 to 14. **The paper's central fiscal verdict turns on that choice.**

## 2. The thesis

### 2.1 Core claim (the two-sided bet)

The financial system is simultaneously holding two exposures that depend on opposite assumptions about the future of human labor:

- **Leg W (wage leg):** long-dated credit underwritten against human labor income. Mortgages, consumer credit, student loans, and sovereign debt serviced from payroll and income tax. Implicitly assumes Physical AI fails or arrives slowly.
- **Leg A (AI leg):** credit and equity extended against AI and robotics capital expenditure. Data centers, robot fleets, automation capex, private credit, AI-linked equity valuations. Implicitly assumes Physical AI succeeds quickly.

The two exposures are jointly compatible with financial stability only over a limited range of adoption speed, realized productivity, and ownership structure.

### 2.2 Centerpiece proposition (the hedge failure)

A holder of both legs is naturally hedged at the extremes. If Physical AI succeeds strongly, Leg A pays and Leg W is impaired. If it fails, the reverse. Partial success (displacement without sufficient surplus, which is Acemoglu's "so-so automation") is the only regime in which both exposures are impaired simultaneously, so it is the only regime in which the natural hedge fails.

Claim this exactly. Do NOT claim partial success is "the most destabilizing scenario". We cannot prove a global ranking without a quantitative model. We can show simultaneous impairment by construction inside the framework.

Corollary: holders of only one leg are unhedged. Regional banks and mortgage lenders hold only Leg W and are exposed in every success scenario. This makes the holder map (Section 5, WS2) the key empirical object.

### 2.3 Formal threshold (draft, you must verify and tighten)

Let:

- dW = wage bill lost to displacement (annual)
- dPi = additional taxable surplus generated by the automation that caused dW
- s = dPi / dW (surplus ratio)
- tau = maximum feasible effective tax or redistribution rate on AI surplus
- d = share of displaced households' income committed to debt service

Regimes:

| Condition | Meaning |
|---|---|
| tau * s >= 1 | Full income replacement is feasible. Problem is political and institutional. |
| d <= tau * s < 1 | Debt service can be preserved, consumption cannot. Demand falls, defaults containable. |
| tau * s < d | No redistribution scheme can keep legacy debt serviced. Problem is arithmetic. Stock-side instruments required. |

"So-so automation" is the region where s is low. This threshold separates the scenarios formally and generates the architecture mapping. Check the derivation, state assumptions explicitly (closed economy first, then note open economy leakage of dPi abroad, which matters for India).

### 2.4 Stocks versus flows

Most proposed remedies (UBI, AI dividends, robot taxes, sovereign funds) repair income flows. The transition instability comes from a stock of legacy debt contracted under wage assumptions. Flow instruments arrive after defaults have hit bank capital. Every architecture recommendation must be tagged as flow-side, stock-side, or buffer-side.

## 4. Hard rules

1. **No fabricated citations.** Every reference must be verified to exist: title, authors, year, venue, DOI or URL. If you cannot verify, mark it `[UNVERIFIED]` and list it in `lit/unverified.md`. A single fake citation ends the paper.
2. **No invented numbers.** Every figure in the sizing table traces to a source file in `data/raw/` with URL, retrieval date, and series ID recorded in `data/SOURCES.md`. Estimates must be labeled as estimates with the method shown.
3. **Reproducible.** One command rebuilds every table and figure from raw data. Python, pinned dependencies, no manual spreadsheet steps.
4. **Claims match evidence.** Use the tiers in Section 3. Hedge only where evidence is missing, and then say what evidence is missing, rather than using vague qualifiers.
5. **Report against the thesis.** If data weakens the thesis, write that up first and prominently in `notes/findings.md`. Do not bury it.
6. **Style.** No em dashes or en dashes anywhere in any output. Direct, terse prose. No filler. State the research question once.
7. **Decisions.** Make architectural and methodological decisions yourself, record them with the tradeoff in `notes/decisions.md`. Ask the owner only when a decision is irreversible or changes the thesis.

### WS0. Systematic literature audit (time-box: 2 weeks)

Goal: confirm or narrow the novelty claim.

- Follow a documented protocol (PRISMA-style): databases searched, query strings, date ranges, inclusion and exclusion criteria, counts at each stage. TFSC reviewers expect this.
- Sources: Scopus or Web of Science if the owner has access, otherwise Google Scholar, SSRN, NBER, arXiv econ and q-fin, RePEc, BIS, IMF, ECB, Fed, Bank of England working paper series.
- Query families: (automation OR robots OR AI) x (household debt, mortgage default, financial stability, bank capital, tax base, fiscal sustainability, stranded assets, AI bubble, capex credit, private credit data centers), plus post-work or post-labor finance, UBI financing, robot tax, sovereign wealth fund AI, income-contingent mortgages, shared-responsibility mortgages.
- Borrow from the climate literature deliberately: "transition risk", "stranded assets", and climate stress testing are the closest methodological analogues. Our framing is transition risk for labor. Cite and adapt it.
- Output: `lit/matrix.csv` (paper, method, channel covered, sectors covered, scenarios, policy, gap relative to us), `lit/audit_report.md`, `lit/references.bib`.

Kill or reshape criterion: if any paper already combines both legs in one exposure framework, stop and report to the owner with a proposed repositioning.

### WS1. Sizing the two legs (time-box: 2 weeks for feasibility, then full build)

Goal: the headline table. Economies: United States, euro area, China, Japan, South Korea. India as a contrast case (Leg W present, domestic Leg A near absent, exposure is imported).

Leg W components and candidate sources:

- Household debt by type: Fed Z.1 Financial Accounts, NY Fed Household Debt and Credit Report, ECB HFCS and sector accounts, BIS total credit statistics, PBoC, BoJ and BoK flow of funds, RBI.
- Distribution of debt across income groups: Fed Distributional Financial Accounts, SCF, HFCS.
- Government labor-tax dependence: OECD Revenue Statistics (personal income tax plus social security contributions plus payroll tax as share of total revenue), national sources for China and India.

Leg A components. There is no standard statistical category for "AI-linked credit", so defining it IS part of the contribution. Build a tiered definition and report all tiers:

- Tier 1 (narrow, hard data): debt issued explicitly to finance AI data centers, GPU-backed lending, robotics fleet financing, data center ABS and CMBS.
- Tier 2 (medium): total capex of hyperscalers and major robotics and automation firms from filings (10-K, annual reports), split by funding source (operating cash flow versus debt versus leases) where disclosed.
- Tier 3 (broad, valuation exposure): market cap attributable to AI expectations. Treat with care. Report as a sensitivity, never in the headline ratio.
- Robotics specifically: IFR World Robotics (paid; ask the owner), company filings, national industrial statistics.

Outputs: `data/processed/sizing_table.csv`, methodology note `notes/sizing_method.md`, figure of Leg W and Leg A as share of GDP by economy.

Kill or reshape criterion: if Tier 1 plus Tier 2 debt-financed Leg A is under roughly 5 percent of Leg W in every economy, the bet is lopsided. Do not hide this. Reframe as an asymmetric exposure: Leg A failure is an equity and private credit event, Leg W failure is systemic. The hedge failure proposition still holds for holders, but the "overinvestment bust" scenario must be downgraded from systemic. Report growth rates of Leg A too, since trajectory may matter more than level.

### WS2. Holder map

Goal: who holds Leg W, who holds Leg A, who holds both.

- Sectors: commercial banks (split large versus regional where possible), nonbank financials (private credit funds, insurers, pension funds), government-sponsored entities, central bank, households (via equity and pensions), rest of world.
- Sources: Z.1 whom-to-whom tables, ECB who-to-whom, FDIC call reports for bank loan book composition, insurer and pension statutory filings, private credit industry reports (label as estimates).
- Output: a holder matrix and a classification of each sector as hedged, unhedged on W, or unhedged on A.

### WS3. Balance-sheet framework

- Five sectors: households (split worker and owner), banks, nonbank financials, firms and AI capital, government. Add rest of world as an extension.
- Build a transactions-flow matrix and balance-sheet matrix in the stock-flow consistent tradition (Godley and Lavoie). Every flow comes from somewhere and goes somewhere. Rows and columns sum to zero.
- For each scenario trace: income shock, balance-sheet shock, default or valuation shock, which sector absorbs the loss, second-round effects.
- Formalize the hedge failure proposition and the tau * s threshold inside this framework. State propositions and assumptions precisely. Proof sketches are enough, but they must be correct.
- Optional, only if time allows: a small numerical SFC simulation (Python) that reproduces the regimes qualitatively. Label it illustrative, not calibrated, unless actually calibrated.

### WS4. Scenario construction

Scenario space, not a list of stories:

- Axis 1: realized Physical AI capability (low, medium, high)
- Axis 2: ownership concentration of AI capital (distributed, concentrated)
- Transition variable: adoption speed (slow, fast), bounded by capital stock turnover and robot manufacturing capacity. Document the bound.

Five named regimes: failure after overinvestment, slow adoption, partial success (so-so), rapid success with broad ownership, rapid success with concentrated ownership. Map each onto the axes and onto the tau * s threshold. Use an established foresight method (morphological analysis or a documented 2x2 plus transition variable) and cite it.

Counterarguments that must be addressed inside the design, not in a closing paragraph: falling prices raise real incomes (but nominal debt is fixed, Fisher debt deflation); capital income is recycled through finance (to whom?); new task creation; adoption speed limits; open economy leakage.

### WS5. Architecture derivation

Rule: failure mechanism determines institution. No menus.

| Failure mechanism | Instrument | Side |
|---|---|---|
| Income volatility during transition | Income-contingent or shared-responsibility debt | Stock |
| Permanent loss of labor income | AI dividends, underwriting on non-labor income | Flow |
| Concentrated AI ownership | Sovereign AI fund, AI rent taxation, broad household equity in AI capital | Flow and ownership |
| Bank concentration in Leg W | Automation-exposure capital add-ons, transition stress tests | Buffer |
| Stranded AI investment | AI capex concentration limits, transition-risk provisioning | Buffer |
| tau * s < d | Legacy debt conversion or restructuring mechanisms | Stock |

For each regime, give the minimum architecture, the precedent (for example Alaska Permanent Fund, Norway GPFG, student loan income-contingent repayment, climate stress tests), and the binding constraint on implementation.

Only after WS0 and WS1 report. Structure: introduction, related literature with audit protocol, framework, scenarios, sizing and holder evidence, architectures, limitations, conclusion. LaTeX, Elsevier template, around 9,000 to 11,000 words. Abstract under 200 words.

## 8. What would make this a lasting paper rather than just an accepted one

No one can guarantee this. These are the levers that are under our control:

1. **One named concept people can repeat.** "The two-sided bet" and "hedge failure under partial success". Keep the names fixed across every document. Do not invent synonyms.
2. **One artifact others must cite.** The sizing table and holder map, released as an open, versioned dataset with a DOI (Zenodo), with a commitment to update it annually. Datasets outlive arguments.
3. **One formal result simple enough to teach.** The tau * s threshold. It should fit on one slide and survive scrutiny. Spend disproportionate effort making it correct and clean.
4. **Falsifiable predictions.** State observable early-warning indicators for each regime (for example: rising delinquency in high physical-exposure occupations while productivity growth stays flat signals the so-so regime). A paper that can be checked against events gets revisited every time events move.
5. **Borrowed legitimacy.** Frame explicitly as the labor analogue of climate transition risk. Regulators already have that machinery (scenario stress tests, NGFS-style scenarios). Give them a template they can adopt with minimal translation. Policy adoption drives citations more than theory does.
6. **Timing.** Central bank research departments are now active here. Post a working paper (SSRN, arXiv econ.GN) as soon as WS0, WS1, WS2 and WS3 are solid, before the journal submission.
7. **Credibility.** The owner should recruit one co-author with macro-finance or central bank credentials and submit to at least one central bank or BIS workshop. Flag candidate names found during WS0 in `notes/open_questions.md`.
8. **Restraint.** No collapse rhetoric. The paper identifies conditions and regimes. Overclaiming is the fastest way to be dismissed by the exact audience that matters.
